# Rule mode: what goes direct and what goes through our lines

Apps use "Rule" mode by default: sites and apps in China connect directly, and only overseas traffic goes through our lines. Leave it at the default; there's nothing to change.

## Why traffic in China should go direct
- **Faster.** Domestic sites are a few hops away; detouring abroad and back only slows them down.
- **Fewer verification checks.** Banks, payment apps and many domestic apps notice where you sign in from. A sudden overseas address can trigger extra checks or even a temporary block.
- **Saves traffic.** Direct traffic doesn't go through our servers and doesn't count against your plan.

Your subscription comes with a rule list: common domestic sites and apps go direct, everything else through our lines.

## The three modes
| Mode | What it does | When to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Rule | Domestic direct, overseas through our lines | All the time |
| Global | Everything through our lines, domestic included | Only briefly, e.g. to test whether a site opens; switch back to Rule afterwards |
| Direct | Nothing through our lines | To turn the lines off for a moment without closing the app |

If a domestic app suddenly slows down or asks you to verify, check you haven't switched to Global by accident. Switch back to Rule, then restart that app.

If a site takes the wrong route (going through our lines when it should go direct, or the reverse), tell us in Tickets and we'll fix the rule for everyone.

## Traffic rate
Traffic is counted in real bytes, upload and download both. Most lines have a rate of 1: 1 GB used counts as 1 GB.

US West lines are forwarded through an entry and an exit server, and have a rate of 1.5: 1 GB used counts as 1.5 GB. Rates depend on our server providers' pricing; if a provider changes its pricing we may adjust, and we'll announce it in the console first. Each server's rate is shown on the Servers page.
